Discussion

What the data mean

The monitor carries real information. It beats plain counting on exactly the faults counting cannot see, on both arrays (Fig. 2).

A real signal is not yet a usable warning. It comes 20 s late and 34 times too often (Figs 3 to 5).

The cause is measured, not guessed. One session holds about one independent measurement, so no threshold or averaging rescues it (Fig. 6).

One question stays open. Deciding less often helps on one array and hurts on the other (Fig. 7). Two arrays cannot settle it.

What is shown, and what is not
  • Shown, on archived recordings only: Figs 2 to 7.
  • Not shown yet: anything on the test bench. Not claimed: any clinical rate or benefit to patients.
  • Limits: two arrays, one bench, one family of monitor.

Conclusion

On archived recordings the monitor detects real faults but warns too late and too often to be relied on. The bench will show whether that belongs to brain recordings or to the method.

Bench, to be measured: lead time s, false alarms per hour.

Next steps
  • Build the bench and pass five acceptance gates before any result is read.
  • Record 337 sessions, each with its fault time sealed first.
  • Report lead time and false alarms together, whatever they are.
