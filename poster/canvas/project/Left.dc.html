01

A decoder fails quietly

An intracortical brain-computer interface turns recorded neural activity into cursor movement through software called a decoder. Over weeks the recording changes: electrodes drift and neurons drop out. The decoder keeps producing confident output from a signal it was not trained on, until it has to be recalibrated, which is slow and disruptive [4]. Methods exist to correct the drift. A warning that it has begun, early enough to act on, has no agreed test.

02

Two quantities decide whether a warning is usable

Lead timeSeconds from the first warning to the moment decoding performance crosses its failure level. Positive means the warning came first.
False-alarm rateWarnings per hour while nothing is wrong. Budget fixed in advance: 0.1 per hour.

The published state of the art validates a decoder-health monitor by its correlation with performance, r = 0.93 and 0.72 [1]. Neither quantity above has been reported for a monitor of this class: both need a recorded moment at which degradation began, and archived recordings do not contain one.

03

Research question

How early, and at what cost in false alarms, can a decoder-health monitor warn that a sensor array is degrading? Does the correlation-based validation now in use support the claims made from it?

04

Hypotheses, frozen before any physical data

Primary: a measurement

For this class of monitor, lead time and false-alarm rate are measurable. Measured against onsets fixed in advance, with enough fault-free recording to bound the rate, they fall outside what a deployed system could use.

Falsified if the median lead time is above zero and the 95% upper bound on the false-alarm rate is within 0.1 per hour, at the threshold needed for 80% detection.

Secondary: a comparison

A monitor can reach the correlation with performance that the field reports as evidence (r above 0.7) while those two quantities say it is unusable.

Falsified if r is above 0.7 together with a positive median lead time and a false-alarm rate within budget. Inconclusive if no correlation measure reaches 0.70.

The monitor: decoder-guard

384 channelslog activity in each 30 s window, one window every 5 s
leveltotal activity has moved
silencechannels have gone quiet

Risk scorelargest component, cause named; warning needs dwell and hysteresis

Fig. 2. Four named components instead of one opaque number. Source: scripts/22_decoder_guard.py.

  • Fitted on healthy windows only. It never sees the decoder output, the task, the performance number or a fault label.
  • Works in log space: a uniform gain change becomes an additive shift and separates from a change in shape across channels.
  • The physical analysis imports this detector and its warning logic unmodified, so a difference between phases cannot come from a retuned monitor.
06

Computational phase: grading built first

ComponentWhat was built
RecordingsPun et al. 2024, public and de-identified [1, 2]. Two implanted arrays, labelled T11 and T5 by the source researchers. No participants were recruited for this project.
Constructed faults1,850 episodes (1,073 on T11, 777 on T5): four fault types, three rates, three severities. Onsets drawn and sealed under SHA-256 before any detector existed.
Reference decoderRidge regression, fitted once and frozen. Median error 54.6° against 90.7° measured chance (T11); 52.8° against 84.9° (T5). Used for grading only.
Evaluation harnessLead time and false-alarm rate reported as a pair. Five gates, silence first. Fit, validation and test split by block; test read once.
Earlier phaseEarly-warning-signal theory [3] under a frozen design: negative. The indicator rose faster during healthy performance than overall.
