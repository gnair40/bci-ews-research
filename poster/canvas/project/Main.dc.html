Seeing Failure Before It Shows

An early-warning monitor for brain-computer interface decoders, and the test bench that shows whether its warnings can be trusted

Gayathri Nair · Central Bucks High School South · [CATEGORY] · [FAIR AND YEAR]

THE WHOLE PROJECT IN ONE MINUTE
1THE PROBLEM
The decoder goes staleAn implant lets a paralysed person move a cursor by intent. The brain signal drifts, and the decoder goes wrong without saying so.
2THE IDEA
Add a warning lightA monitor watches the signal itself and warns before control is lost, so the user recalibrates only when it is needed.
3THE CATCH
Test the warningA warning is only useful if it comes early and is rarely wrong. For this kind of monitor, neither had been measured.
4THIS PROJECT
Build the testOne monitor and two test beds where the moment of failure is fixed in advance: brain recordings with planted faults, and a camera-and-screen bench.

Materials and methods

How the test bench works

1The screen shows 384 patches whose brightness encodes a known direction.
2A camera films them at 50 frames a second, in the dark.
3A decoder, fitted once and then frozen, reads the direction back from the video.
4The monitor scores risk every 5 s from the last 30 s of signal.
5A fault begins at a moment drawn and sealed before recording.
6Each warning is scored against that sealed moment: how early, and how many false alarms.
