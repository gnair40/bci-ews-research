Seeing Failure Before It Shows

An early-warning monitor for brain-computer interface decoders, validated on archived brain recordings and on a purpose-built optical test bench

Introduction: start hereNearly 5.4 million people in the United States live with paralysis [3]

A decoder left unchanged worked for about two months, then failed (Fig. 1). This project tests whether such a failure can be seen coming, early enough and reliably enough to act on.

1The problem · established science

A decoder tuned to yesterday

Fig. 1. Real data [1], array T11: median cursor error by session, one decoder kept for 142 days.

In research trials, people with paralysis steer a cursor by intent: implanted electrodes record brain activity and software called a decoder translates it [1]. The recording keeps changing [7]. The decoder still expects the old pattern, so control degrades, and nothing tells the user.

A warning would not stop the drift. It would let the repair happen only when needed, before control is lost. But a warning has to be trusted: in hospitals 72 to 99% of monitor alarms are false [4], and nuisance alarms are the leading reason smoke alarms are disconnected [5].

3This project · shown on archived data

How early, and how often wrong

Earlier work corrects drift automatically [2, 6], or scores it and reports a correlation with performance, r = 0.93 and 0.72 [1]. No published lead time or false-alarm rate was found. This project plants 1,850 faults in real recordings at start times sealed in advance, then measures both.

Physical validationpreregistered, not built

Archived data cannot supply a recorded start time or enough fault-free hours. A bench can: its faults begin at sealed moments and it records unattended. The apparatus is the subject; no person is measured.

1Screen: 384 patches whose brightness encodes a known direction.
2Camera: films them at 50 frames a second, in the dark.
3Decoder: fitted once on fault-free sessions, then frozen.
4Monitor: the same code as on the brain recordings.
5Fault: filter, rotation or software; begins at a sealed moment.
6Scoring: each warning against that sealed moment.

Analysis

Why: one measurement, 720 decisions

Risk scores within a session are near-copies of each other, so 720 decisions an hour rest on about one independent measurement. Deciding once per session would still need a score of 0.990.
